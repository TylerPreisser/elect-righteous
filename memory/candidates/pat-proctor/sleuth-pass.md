## Primary-Source Anchors

- **Campaign statement / issue frame:** Proctor's campaign homepage leads with "Restoring Confidence in Our Elections, Growing Our Economy." It says the Secretary of State has two primary duties: securing and accurately executing elections, and facilitating business formation and operation. It says he has "championed and enacted into law" safeguards including only U.S. citizens voting, Election Day as the deadline for casting ballots, and paper audits.  
  Source: https://patproctor4ks.com/
- **Election administration role:** Kansas Legislature lists Proctor as chair of House Elections and chair of the Joint Committee on Kansas Security.  
  Source: https://www.kslegislature.gov/li/b2025_26/members/rep_proctor_pat_1/
- **Federal election-administration advisory role:** The U.S. Election Assistance Commission Board of Advisors roster dated February 24, 2026 lists "Representative Pat Proctor, Kansas House of Representatives" as a National Conference of State Legislatures designee.  
  Source: https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/2026-02/BOA_Roster_for_Website_022426.pdf
- **Citizenship-voting amendment action:** Kansas Legislature lists Proctor among sponsors of HCR 5004, a constitutional amendment requiring voters to be U.S. citizens, at least 18, and residents of the voting area. His 2025 House vote record shows he voted yea on final action on February 5, 2025; the House adopted it 90-28, and the Senate adopted it 37-3.  
  Sources: https://www.kslegislature.gov/li/b2025_26/measures/hcr5004/ and https://www.kslegislature.gov/b2025_26/resolutions/HCR5004/history/?filter=votes&per_page=20
- **Advance-ballot deadline action:** HB 2453 would have required advance-ballot applications by the 14th day before Election Day, ended in-person advance voting at noon on the Sunday before Election Day, transmitted advance ballots 22 days before an election, and ended voter registration 23 days before Election Day. House roll call #0094 shows Proctor voted yea on February 18, 2026; the bill passed the House 86-38 and died in Senate committee on April 10, 2026.  
  Sources: https://www.kslegislature.gov/li/b2025_26/measures/hb2453/ and https://www.kslegislature.gov/b2025_26/bills/HB2453/history/
- **Foreign-national constitutional-amendment campaign finance action:** HB 2106 banned contributions from foreign nationals for support or defeat of proposed Kansas constitutional amendments. It was sponsored by the House Elections Committee, requested by Rep. Waggoner, passed the House 94-25, passed the Senate 39-0, and became law without the governor's signature on April 10, 2025. Proctor's 2025 House vote record shows he voted yea on February 14, 2025.  
  Sources: https://www.kslegislature.gov/li/b2025_26/measures/hb2106/ and https://www.kslegislature.gov/b2025_26/bills/HB2106/history/?filter=votes&per_page=20
- **Gun-rights resolution sponsorship:** HCR 5006 proposed a constitutional amendment treating the right to bear arms, including ammunition and firearm accessories/components, as a fundamental right subject to strict scrutiny. Kansas Legislature lists Proctor as an original/current sponsor. It died on the House calendar on April 10, 2026 without a recorded vote.  
  Source: https://www.kslegislature.gov/li/b2025_26/measures/hcr5006/
- **Campaign finance totals:** Proctor's January 2026 KPDC report covers 2025-01-01 through 2025-12-31. It reports $312,046.74 in contributions and other receipts, $102,663.46 in expenditures, $209,383.28 cash on hand, $2,650.22 in in-kind contributions, and $134,000.00 in other transactions. Schedule D itemizes loans from Patrick Proctor to the campaign.  
  Source: https://www.kansas.gov/ethics/CFAScanned/StWide/2026ElecCycle/202601/SW04PP_202601.pdf
