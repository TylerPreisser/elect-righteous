## Issue-Mapping Notes

- **Insurance affordability / consumer protection:** strongest current campaign issue. Candidate-controlled site says the office should put people first, lower costs, increase transparency, and hold insurance companies accountable. Kansas Reflector launch coverage quotes Sykes saying the cost of health insurance is out of control and that she would not take insurance-industry money.
- **Campaign finance / regulator independence:** directly tied to the insurance issue. Campaign and launch coverage state she pledged not to take insurance-industry contributions. KPDC confirms campaign setup but no visible receipts report yet, so donor patterns cannot be tested.
- **Working-family affordability:** official legislative materials show Sykes as sponsor/co-sponsor on paid sick time, minimum wage, child tax credit, property-tax, and Social Security income-tax bills. Governor's-office release quotes her supporting a $15 minimum wage.
- **Health care access / Medicaid / preventive care:** campaign and caucus materials emphasize health-care costs and Medicaid expansion. Official bill list includes breast-cancer screening coverage legislation.
- **Public schools / education:** Kansas Senate Democrats profile says she has focused on public-school funding and early childhood education. Official 2025 vote record shows she voted against SB 75, a tax-credit/school-choice bill, and HB 2045, an education savings account bill.
- **Abortion / reproductive policy:** 2025 official vote record shows she voted against SB 63, which regulated abortion coercion and related reporting/ultrasound matters and became law after veto override activity. Secondary coverage also documents reproductive-rights rally remarks. Present neutrally as opposition to abortion restrictions and support for reproductive-rights access.
- **LGBTQ policy:** official 2025 vote record shows she voted against SB 63 in the motion listed on her vote record and against SB 76 concerning eligibility for public benefits related to "gender reassignment service." Secondary and social-harvest sources document public statements opposing anti-trans legislation. Do not infer a marriage-policy position from party label.

A 2023 candidate-attributed opinion described her as a Christian, mother, and daughter who lost her mother to breast cancer. Use only as self-description in that article; do not certify faith identity or infer policy from it.
