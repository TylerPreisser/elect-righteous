## Status Summary

Charlotte O'Hara is running an active Republican campaign for Kansas governor. Her campaign homepage identifies the race as the 2026 Kansas gubernatorial primary. Kansas Public Disclosure Commission/Kansas.gov statewide 2026 election-cycle records list "O'Hara, Charlotte" under gubernatorial candidates with Appointment of Treasurer, amendment, and 202601 report links.

Official ballot wording needs caution. The Kansas SOS candidate-information page says statewide candidates file with the Secretary of State and are not considered filed until the petition or declaration and fee are received. The same page lists the 2026 primary filing deadline as 12:00 p.m. Monday, June 1, 2026. This pass did not obtain a machine-readable official SOS filed-candidate list confirming final ballot placement. Treat O'Hara as an active gubernatorial campaign/finance filer, with final filed-candidate status pending official readback.

O'Hara's public record includes prior Kansas House service and Johnson County Commission service. The Kansas Legislature archive lists her as the 2011-2012 Republican representative for District 27, first term 2011. Johnson County Election Office's profile lists County Commissioner District 3, term end 2025, and "Elected Official: No" on the page as accessed in 2026. Official Johnson County final 2024 results show she lost the District 3 commissioner race to Julie Brewer.

## Issue Area 1 - Taxes, Spending, Incentives, and Property Taxes

### Candidate-controlled platform

**Sources:** s-campaign-home, s-property-tax-shift.

The homepage lists "Rein in spending," "Stop tax incentives," and "Demand Transparency" among the reasons she says she is running. It says tax incentives increase property taxes and says "DOGE must come to Kansas."

In "The Great Property Tax Shift" (2026-05-05), O'Hara argues Johnson County residential owners carry a much larger share of property-tax revenue than in 1989. The post criticizes STAR bonds, TIFs, IRBs, HPIP, and PEAK, names the Chiefs stadium package and Hallbrook North as examples, and says she would propose legislation to prohibit local governments from hiring lobbyists, sunset selected incentive programs, stop tax exemptions/credits, and eliminate property taxes through spending cuts and audits.

### Action evidence from Johnson County records

**Source:** s-joco-june-2023.

Johnson County's official June 2023 recap records O'Hara voting no on:

- Publication of the proposed FY2024 county budget, passed 5-2.
- Pay-table changes and market pay adjustment, passed 5-2.
- Creation of Sustainability Coalition, passed 5-2.
- Creation of Diversity, Equity and Inclusion Coalition, passed 5-2.
- CNANO/New Century Commerce Center taxable industrial revenue bonds up to $34,150,000 and 10-year ad valorem tax abatement/PILOT, passed 5-1 with O'Hara as the only no vote.
- Several planning/zoning and Sunflower Fire Station funding items.
- Opioid settlement allocation to UCS of Johnson County and Johnson County Prevention and Recovery Coalition, passed 5-1.

**Issue mapping:** taxes/spending/incentives; fiscal governance; DEI coalition vote; development incentives.

**Neutral framing:** These are votes against county actions. Do not infer motive; pair votes with candidate's stated tax/incentive rationale where appropriate.

## Issue Area 3 - Elections, Voting, Transparency, and Judicial Selection

### Campaign platform and posts

**Sources:** s-campaign-home, s-election-integrity-post, s-bar-association-post, s-campaign-archive, s-campaign-categories.

The homepage calls for direct partisan election of judges and says the Kansas Bar Association's role in judicial selection should end. It also lists transparency as a major campaign point.

"Election Integrity: the Ghost of 2020" (2026-03-24) ties election-integrity claims to a sports-authority/STAR bond fight, endorses the SAVE Act to require proof of citizenship, opposes universal mail ballots, and ends with "Paper Ballots, anyone?"

"We the People vs. the Kansas Bar Association" (2026-05-19) urges support for an August 4 primary ballot constitutional amendment for direct election of Kansas Supreme Court justices. The post asks pastors and priests to support the amendment and frames the court-selection issue through abortion and the Kansas Supreme Court.

The campaign-site categories on 2026-05-19 show 52 posts categorized as Elections, 47 as Voting, and 59 as Transparency out of 73 posts. Categories overlap.

### Public testimony / official records

**Sources:** s-ksleg-hb2206, s-ksleg-hb2206-testimony, s-ksleg-sb366-minutes, s-joco-koma.

HB 2206's official page lists O'Hara as a private-citizen opponent/in-person presenter on Mar. 12, 2025. Her written testimony argued lawmakers should allow ethics investigations to proceed before reforming campaign-finance law.

Senate Federal and State Affairs minutes for Jan. 24, 2024 list Charlotte O'Hara, then a Johnson County commissioner, as proponent oral testimony for SB 366, a bill about county election officers mailing advance-ballot applications only after voter request. The minutes summarize her cost and ballot-security concerns about Johnson County's advance-ballot application mailer.

Johnson County's Aug. 22, 2023 notice says O'Hara filed a KOMA complaint with the AG alleging BOCC violations in a June 29, 2023 executive session. The county notice says four of five allegations were found not to be violations and closed, while the AG requested more information on one remaining allegation.

**Issue mapping:** election administration; campaign-finance transparency; open meetings/government process; judicial selection.
