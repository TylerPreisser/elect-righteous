## Status Summary

Vicki Schmidt is the sitting Kansas Insurance Commissioner and an active Republican candidate for governor. The Kansas Insurance Department biography identifies her as Kansas's 27th insurance commissioner, first elected in 2018 and reelected in 2022. It also identifies her as a University of Kansas pharmacy graduate, longtime pharmacist, former state senator, and the first pharmacist to hold statewide elected office in Kansas.

Kansas Public Disclosure Commission/Kansas.gov statewide 2026 records list "Schmidt, Vicki" under gubernatorial candidates with an Appointment of Treasurer link. The Appointment of Treasurer form lists office sought as Governor, treasurer Brad Koehn, and execution date July 9, 2025. A direct Kansas.gov/KPDC 202601 report PDF for Vicki L. Schmidt lists office sought as Governor and reports 2025 receipts, expenditures, cash on hand, in-kind contributions, and other transactions.

Official ballot wording needs caution. The Kansas Secretary of State candidate-information page says candidates for national, state, legislative, and judicial offices must file with the Secretary of State and are not considered filed until their petition or declaration and fee are received. It lists the 2026 primary filing deadline as 12:00 p.m. Monday, June 1, 2026. The official SOS candidate-list endpoint returned a JavaScript/robot-verification page in text capture. Schmidt's May 18, 2026 campaign announcement says she and running mate Joe Newland "will officially file for office together on June 1." Treat her as an active gubernatorial campaign/finance filer with final SOS candidate-list/ballot status pending official readback.

## Election and Office Records

### Current office and biography

**Sources:** s-kdoi-about, s-campaign-home.

The Kansas Insurance Department biography identifies Schmidt as the 27th Kansas Insurance Commissioner, first elected in 2018 and reelected in 2022. It says she was born and raised in Wichita, graduated from the University of Kansas School of Pharmacy, worked as a local pharmacist for more than 40 years, served in the Kansas Senate for 14 years, and is the first pharmacist to hold statewide elected office in Kansas.

The campaign site uses the same biography to present Schmidt as a pharmacist, former state senator, sitting statewide executive, breast-cancer survivor, wife, mother, and grandmother.

**Issue mapping:** biography, public office, health-care background, executive experience.

### 2026 campaign, filing posture, and running mate

**Sources:** s-sos-candidate-info, s-sos-candidate-list-blocked, s-kpdc-statewide-index, s-kpdc-at-gov, s-campaign-newland.

The SOS candidate-information page lists Governor/Lt. Governor among 2026 offices and says candidates file with SOS and are not considered filed until petition/declaration and fee are received. The listed primary filing deadline is noon on June 1, 2026.

KPDC statewide 2026 records list Schmidt under gubernatorial candidates and link her Appointment of Treasurer. The AT form identifies Vicki L. Schmidt as seeking Governor and names Brad Koehn as treasurer.

On May 18, 2026, the campaign announced Kansas Farm Bureau President Joe Newland as lieutenant-governor selection. The release says the Schmidt/Newland ticket will officially file together on June 1.

**Issue mapping:** election status, running mate, rural/agriculture.

**Caution:** Because the SOS candidate-list endpoint was blocked and the campaign says filing will occur June 1, downstream copy should avoid saying "certified ballot candidate" until official readback is obtained.

## Issue Area 1 - Insurance Consumer Protection and Executive Record

**Sources:** s-kdoi-2025-recoveries, s-kdoi-about, s-campaign-home, s-social-harvest-local.

The strongest official record is insurance consumer assistance. The Jan. 15, 2026 Department release says the Department recovered $56.7 million for Kansans in 2025 and more than $202.7 million since Schmidt took office in 2019. Schmidt said, "The work we do consistently puts more money back in the pockets of Kansans," and the release said recoveries came from consumer assistance, market regulation, and anti-fraud work.

The campaign site makes the same competence argument in broader campaign terms. It says Schmidt found errors in the Kansas Medical Assistance Program that produced more than $391 million in recouped dollars for the state, returned over $206 million to Kansas families as insurance commissioner, and lowered the cost of doing business by more than $96 million.

The local social harvest found that insurance consumer assistance, recoveries, claims help, storm-damage guidance, fraud/scam prevention, and securities education were the dominant accessible official-channel topics.

**Issue mapping:** insurance consumer protection, executive management, fraud/scam prevention, health-care administration.

**Evidence caution:** Use Department release totals for primary-source claims. Campaign-site broader totals are candidate-controlled and should be attributed as campaign claims unless independently verified line-by-line.

## Issue Area 2 - Fees, Cost Reduction, and Agency Administration

**Sources:** s-kdoi-fee-2026, s-hb2050, s-campaign-home.

The Nov. 20, 2025 Department release says Schmidt announced a 2026 fee schedule that would reduce fees by an estimated $1.1 million. It says this is the seventh year of lowering costs at the Department and says consumers will see reductions in specific producer, agency, and continuing-education fees.

HB 2050 is the official legislative anchor for Department fee modernization. The official Legislature page says the bill concerns certain fees and operations of the Insurance Department, lists the Department as requester through Eric Turek, and shows the bill was approved by the governor on April 23, 2025.

The campaign site frames this as part of Schmidt's case for government efficiency and reduced business costs.

**Issue mapping:** government efficiency, agency fees, cost of doing business.

**Evidence caution:** HB 2050 is Department-requested action evidence, not a personal legislative vote by Schmidt. Describe it as Department-requested legislation and agency implementation under Schmidt's office.

## Issue Area 3 - Prescription Drug Costs and PBM Regulation

**Sources:** s-kdoi-pbm, s-sb360, s-social-harvest-local.

On Feb. 12, 2026, the Department said Schmidt testified before the Senate Financial Institutions and Insurance Committee in support of SB 360, described as PBM reform. The release quotes Schmidt saying PBMs should not threaten patients, local businesses, or rural communities and that lawmakers should protect Kansas patients and independent pharmacists.

The official SB 360 page says the bill would enact the Community Pharmacy Protection Act and amend law on pharmacy benefits managers. It records Senate passage 32-8 on Feb. 26, 2026, House referral to Insurance, House committee report recommending passage, and later rereferral to Health and Human Services.

The local social harvest recorded the Department LinkedIn page as visibly emphasizing PBM reform among official-channel social topics.

**Issue mapping:** prescription drug costs, pharmacies, rural health, insurance regulation.

**Evidence caution:** Official bill status should control over secondary shorthand. As of the official page captured May 19, the bill had not been shown as enacted.

## Issue Area 4 - Taxes, Property Taxes, Affordability, and Fiscal Framing

**Sources:** s-campaign-reflector-tax-abortion, s-kvoe-interview, s-campaign-home.

Schmidt's campaign-amplified July 21, 2025 Kansas Reflector interview/article includes her statement on the 2012 Brownback-era tax vote: "It is a vote I regret." The same repost frames her campaign around record of service and executive experience.

In an April 23, 2026 KVOE interview, Schmidt identified property taxes as a top issue and said education funding and local government funding were contributing factors. KVOE also reported her saying business development and housing would be economic priorities, and that she supports reviewing Medicaid expansion but prefers a Kansas-driven solution.

The campaign home page frames affordability through hard work, efficiency, recouped money, and reduced costs rather than a detailed tax plan.

**Issue mapping:** taxes, property taxes, affordability, economic development, Medicaid expansion.

**Evidence caution:** This pass did not capture an official roll call for the 2012 tax vote or a detailed campaign tax platform. Treat current material mostly as stated-position evidence.

## Issue Area 6 - Rural Kansas, Agriculture, Water, and Running-Mate Signal

**Sources:** s-campaign-newland, s-campaign-meet-joe, s-campaign-home.
