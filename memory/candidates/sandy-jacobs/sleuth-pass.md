## Issue-Mapped Observations

### Observation 1 - Off-cycle incumbent status is now verified

**What was observed:** City of Hays lists Jacobs as a commissioner; Ellis County's official result path confirms the 2025 city/school election; January 2026 minutes show she was sworn in again and moved from mayor back to commissioner; current 2026 county filings do not list a Hays City Commission race.

**Issue mapping:** Official status / election-cycle accuracy.

**Source tier:** Primary for city page, minutes, county election-results page/PDF, and 2026 filing PDF; Hays Post is used for readable term/vote narrative.

### Observation 2 - Water/R9 is her clearest long-term issue

**What was observed:** In her 2025 Hays Post Q&A, Jacobs named R9 as unfinished work she wanted to see through and said Hays had asked for less water than state rules allow. The City of Hays R9 page frames the ranch as a long-term sustainable water-supply asset. Local coverage also ties Jacobs to water-conservation and R9 updates.

**Issue mapping:** Water security; R9 Ranch; long-term infrastructure.

**Source tier:** Primary for official project context; secondary for candidate-attributed statements.

### Observation 3 - Budget language is conservative but tradeoff-aware

**What was observed:** Official September 2025 minutes show the city found a path to remain revenue neutral at 24.382 mills and that maintaining revenue-neutral status would not affect operations or capital projects. Jacobs voted aye on the budget action. In the 2025 Q&A she praised conservative budgeting, said the city was not overtaxing from her perspective, and said the first goal is to remain revenue neutral. In April 2026 coverage she asked where new money would come from and what people would be willing to give up.

**Issue mapping:** Taxes; city budget; service tradeoffs; fiscal stewardship.

**Source tier:** Primary for official budget action; secondary for quotes.

### Observation 4 - Housing and low-income housing are supported through incentives

**What was observed:** Jacobs' Q&A credited housing as a major commission accomplishment, including economic-development policy changes and RHIDs. April 2026 agenda materials show the city supporting fee waivers for eligible Low-Income Housing Tax Credit projects. Tiger Media reported the Pioneer Run IRB passed 5-0 and quoted Jacobs saying low-income housing is difficult to make feasible without LIHTC assistance. Hays Post reported on May 18, 2026 that commissioners unanimously agreed to two more low-income housing IRB resolutions and quoted Jacobs saying, "Let's hope they get it."

**Issue mapping:** Housing; development incentives; LIHTC; affordability.

**Source tier:** Primary for agenda materials; secondary for candidate quote and vote summary.

### Observation 5 - Economic development support is concrete, especially retail and corridor growth

**What was observed:** Jacobs says retail development is a top goal beyond maintaining taxes. In February 2026 travel-plaza coverage she called the I-70 project exciting and linked it to alternate routes into town. In March 2026 Chick-fil-A coverage she tied the project to the Vine Street roundabout investments, and March 26 minutes show she seconded the Chick-fil-A IRB resolution, which passed unanimously.

**Issue mapping:** Retail development; incentives; infrastructure return; sales-tax base.

**Source tier:** Primary for Chick-fil-A action; secondary for quotes and project descriptions.

### Observation 6 - Infrastructure and code votes show operations-level governance

**What was observed:** March 26, 2026 minutes show Jacobs voting aye on sanitary sewer cleaning and inspection, moving centrifuge maintenance, seconding the 2024 International Fire Code adoption, moving the 2024 International Property Maintenance Code adoption, and seconding the 2023 National Electrical Code adoption. These actions fit her public emphasis on taking care of city assets and infrastructure.

**Issue mapping:** Infrastructure maintenance; public safety codes; city operations.

**Source tier:** Primary.

### Observation 7 - COVID mask vote should be described narrowly

**What was observed:** Hays Post reported that the October 2020 special-meeting mask ordinance extension passed 3-1, with Jacobs, Shaun Musil, and Michael Berges voting yes and Mason Ruder voting no. Existing local notes say Jacobs voted with the majority to extend the ordinance. Keep this as a COVID-era local public-health ordinance vote, not a broad inference about later public-health views.

**Issue mapping:** COVID-era public health mandate.

**Source tier:** Secondary unless underlying 2020 minutes are separately pulled.
