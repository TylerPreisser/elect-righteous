## Issue Mapping Notes

### Water / R9

Jacobs' strongest issue lane is long-term water security. Candidate-attributed evidence comes from her 2025 Hays Post Q&A, where she named R9 as unfinished work and defended the city's legal/statutory posture. Official context comes from the City R9 Ranch page, which presents the project as a long-term sustainable water-supply investment. Avoid writing as if every R9 institutional statement is Jacobs' personal view; tie her to direct quotes and her commission role.

### Budget / Taxes

Official September 2025 minutes are the best action anchor. The city found a path to stay revenue-neutral at 24.382 mills, and Jacobs voted aye. Her Q&A language is consistent with this: first goal revenue neutral, conservative budget, not overtaxing from her perspective. April 2026 budget-prep coverage adds tradeoff language but should be treated as a meeting quote, not a completed tax vote.

### Housing / LIHTC / Incentives

Housing is a major stated accomplishment in the 2025 Q&A. The best current action evidence is April-May 2026: fee-waiver letters, Pioneer Run IRB, Willow Grove and Wheatland Commons IRB resolutions, and Jacobs' quotes that LIHTC help is needed and "Let's hope they get it." May 14 minutes were not yet visible, so keep that action source as Hays Post plus agenda packet until minutes post.

### Retail / Economic Development

Jacobs explicitly names retail development as a top goal after maintaining taxes. She supported the travel-plaza concept at the February 2026 work session, seconded the March 2026 Chick-fil-A IRB resolution, and tied Vine Street roundabouts to development access. Note that she was absent from the April 9 travel-plaza final vote according to April 23 packet minutes, so do not attribute that vote to her.

### Infrastructure / Codes / Operations

March 26, 2026 minutes are dense with Jacobs-specific actions: sewer cleaning, centrifuge maintenance, fire code, property maintenance code, electrical code, UDC carport changes, and the Chick-fil-A IRB. These provide issue evidence for practical city operations and asset stewardship.

### Public Health / COVID

The 2020 mask extension should be narrowly framed. Hays Post reports Jacobs voted yes on an extension. Do not use this to infer broad views on vaccines, federal policy, later public-health mandates, or personal health beliefs.
