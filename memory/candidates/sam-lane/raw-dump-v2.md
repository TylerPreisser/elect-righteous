### Home page
Source: https://www.samuellane.org/

Findings:
- Page identifies him as "Sam Lane for Kansas Secretary of State."
- Candidate statement says he is a lifelong Kansan and lifelong independent running as a Democrat for Secretary of State.

- Footer says the site is paid for by Samuel Lane for Kansas, Erika Ashley treasurer.

Issue relevance:

### What does the Secretary of State do?
Source: https://www.samuellane.org/what-does-the-secretary-of-state-do

Findings:
- The page says the Kansas Secretary of State has several duties, mostly administrative, including registering businesses for state taxes and publishing legal paperwork.
- It says the Secretary of State is also charged with overseeing elections in Kansas.
- It frames election integrity as a partisan-political front and says the office should keep elections fair and accurate.
- It says the Secretary of State is on the front line of defending democracy.
- It states: "We can't allow voter suppression. We can't allow voter fraud." The page closes with "We hold the line."

Issue relevance:
- Election administration and election security.
- Business services mentioned, but not developed into a detailed policy program in this source.

### Issues page
Source: https://www.samuellane.org/issues

Findings by section:

Voter fraud:
- The page references Heritage Foundation counts of voter-fraud cases in Kansas since 2005 and says most are duplicate voting.
- It says current Secretary of State Scott Schwab maintains that Kansas does not have a voter-fraud or election-security problem, and Lane says evidence indicates he is correct.
- The page still says systems should be reviewed and maintained.
- It says Lane would seek "a thorough and unbroken paper trail" at every election site for every vote.
- It says paper ballots cannot be hacked in the way online-only voting could be.
- It notes he has been impressed with county election offices visited so far, and says an election commissioner suggested demonstrating full logic and accuracy testing before processing actual ballots.

Voter suppression / HB 2438:
- The page links Kansas HB 2438 and describes it as placing new restrictions on websites used for voter registration and charging the Secretary of State with keeping a "safe" list updated.
- It says such measures can look like common-sense security but could become registration chokepoints if the next Secretary of State neglected the list.
- Official cross-check: Kansas Legislature HB 2438 short title says it imposes restrictions on websites that can accept and transmit voter-registration applications. Source: https://www.kslegislature.gov/li/b2025_26/measures/hb2438/

Mail ballots / SB 4:
- The page addresses ending the three-day grace period for mail-in ballots.
- It says voters should probably plan on voting in person in 2026 if possible; if voting by mail, it advises using a drop box or mailing promptly.
- Official cross-check: Kansas Legislature SB 4 says it requires return of advance voting ballots by 7:00 p.m. on election day and was veto-overridden. Source: https://www.kslegislature.gov/li/b2025_26/measures/sb4/

SAVE Act / SAVE America Act / Make Elections Great Again Act:
- The page links federal bill pages and says these bills deal with citizenship documentation, in-person registration, and mail ballots.
- The page says Lane would try to ensure Kansas voter registration is up to date if federal requirements change.
- The page connects documentation rules to transgender voters and married-name changes.

SB 244 / identity documents:
- The page discusses Kansas SB 244 in the context of driver's-license sex-marker changes and voter registration documentation.
- Official cross-check: Kansas Legislature SB 244 page says House Substitute for SB 244 requires sex-designated multi-occupancy private spaces in public buildings, defines gender as biological sex at birth for statutory construction, and directs driver-license and birth-certificate correction/reissuance when necessary; the bill was veto-overridden. Source: https://www.kslegislature.gov/li/b2025_26/measures/sb244/

Ballot-location security and closures:
- The page says Kansas counties pay for their own elections with limited state support, and that rural and urban counties have different budgets.
- It says Lane wants unobtrusive security around ballot drop boxes, tactful plainclothes security at ballot locations, and plans for bomb threats.
- It says fewer ballot locations can mean longer trips, crowds, and lines, with greater effect on working people, people without cars, and rural voters.
- It says churches and other polling places sign up voluntarily, so the Secretary of State cannot simply command availability.

Issue relevance:
- Strong relevance to Secretary of State authority and county election administration.
- Includes broader civil-liberties concerns, but the page generally ties them back to voter registration, ballots, and election infrastructure.

### My Other Views
Source: https://www.samuellane.org/my-other-views

Findings:
- Page says the content is not as immediately relevant to the Secretary of State office, but is offered so voters know the candidate.
- Abortion tab: Lane says he does not like to dwell on personal feelings about abortion; he says abortion policy is the less complicated part for him; he criticizes abortion bans for effects on medical practice and emergency care.
- Guns tab: Lane says he does not make an exception for gun rights when talking about civil liberties. He says if he were running for a lawmaker office he would be alright with some basic gun-control legislation such as closing background-check loopholes, but notes he is not running for a lawmaker office.

Issue relevance:
