## Issue-Mapping Notes

- **Public education / school funding:** Strongest record-and-message overlap. Campaign biography emphasizes 2016 Shawnee-to-Topeka walks over school funding, public-school graduates in her family, and legislative work after the Brownback tax experiment. Official legislative record shows Holscher sponsored SB 142, authorizing districts to include a nonvoting teacher representative on boards of education.
- **Affordability / taxes / labor:** Campaign priorities and Feb. 3, 2026 release lay out a "Common Sense and Lower Costs" agenda: minimum wage, paid sick time, repeal right-to-work, property-tax relief, Medicaid expansion, free school lunches, essential-products tax relief, special-education funding, housing security, medical marijuana, redistricting, term limits, and corporate PAC limits. Legislative vote record includes yes votes on rural opportunity zone benefits and a 2025 property-tax/homestead-related package.
- **Healthcare / Medicaid:** Campaign priorities call for Medicaid expansion and connect expansion to rural hospitals. Legislature lists Holscher as ranking minority member on Public Health and Welfare. Social/campaign posts emphasize ACA subsidies and healthcare affordability.
- **Reproductive care / abortion:** KMUW/Kansas Reflector forum coverage reported Holscher supported reproductive healthcare access. Legislative vote record shows she voted nay on HB 2062, a 2025 bill on child support orders for unborn children and unborn/stillborn income-tax exemptions. The captured Bluesky feed did not make abortion a high-frequency original topic.
- **Democracy / redistricting / campaign finance:** Campaign priorities call for an independent redistricting panel, prohibition on mid-decade redistricting, term limits, and corporate PAC limits. Legislative record shows Holscher sponsored SB 136 on limits/reporting/audits for independent-expenditure political committees and SB 143 on legislative term limits. Vote record shows nay votes on SB 4 advance-ballot deadline changes and SB 5 restrictions on federal election funds.
- **Immigration / CoreCivic / private detention:** Campaign news page includes March 2026 releases opposing the Leavenworth CoreCivic/ICE detention project. Bluesky post from March 20, 2026 says she would not take CoreCivic money. A text search of the 2025 KPDC report did not find the literal strings "CoreCivic" or "Core Civic"; this does not rule out all affiliated entities without a full donor-origin audit.
- **Marriage, family, and LGBTQ policy:** Vote record shows Holscher voted nay on SB 63, the 2025 gender-transition-care restriction bill, and nay on SB 76, the school name/pronoun bill. No candidate-controlled marriage-specific plank was found.
- **Guns / Second Amendment:** No candidate-controlled gun-policy plank was found in reviewed campaign pages or the measured Bluesky corpus. Vote record includes many public-safety votes, but no clean, candidate-stated gun-policy issue card was confirmed in this pass.
- **Agriculture / rural economy / tariffs:** Campaign biography and social feed connect farm background to rural cost pressure, tariffs, crop markets, and rural hospitals. This is mostly campaign/social/news evidence rather than a discrete gubernatorial agricultural platform page.
- **Medical marijuana / drug policy:** Campaign priorities support legalizing medical marijuana and studying recreational marijuana effects in neighboring states.
- **Religious liberty / faith:** Campaign biography and official legislative/campaign pages reviewed here did not identify a current church affiliation. Social harvest found occasional faith or religious-community references, but no religious-liberty policy plank.
