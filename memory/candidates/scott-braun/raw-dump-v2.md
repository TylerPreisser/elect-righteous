## Official Status / Election Cycle

Ellis County's official 2024 general election results list "REP Scott J Braun" for county sheriff with 11,797 votes, while write-ins received 117. Hays Post election-night coverage reported a preliminary 11,524 figure. Use the official county total in final data.

The public profile should be framed as incumbent/current Ellis County Sheriff, Republican, re-elected 2024. The next regular sheriff election is expected in 2028 because Kansas county offices run on four-year terms in the even-year county cycle. This pass did not locate a 2026 filing for Braun.

## Biography / Community Background

Hays Post's 2019 announcement says Braun graduated from Victoria High School, attended Barton County Community College and Fort Hays State University, began as a reserve deputy in 1995, worked for the Ellis Police Department, graduated from the Kansas Law Enforcement Training Center in 1996, joined the Ellis County Sheriff's Office in 1997, worked in the drug enforcement unit, spearheaded the county canine program in 1999, became narcotics detective in 2005, and was appointed undersheriff in 2018.

The same announcement and LinkedIn profile tie Braun to Hays Rotary, St. Nicholas of Myra Church Council, Knights of Columbus, First Call for Help, Kansas Narcotics Officer Association, Ellis County Crisis Intervention Team, and Big Brothers Big Sisters. Treat these as civic/professional identity signals, not automatic policy proxies.

## Campaign Finance / Donor Information

No online itemized donor list, receipts/expenditures report, appointment of treasurer, or affidavit of exemption for Braun was found in this pass.

KPDC says the Campaign Finance Act covers county offices and that local candidates file Appointment of Treasurer forms with the county clerk or county election commissioner. Covered candidates must file either an affidavit of exemption or periodic receipts/expenditures reports. That means any Braun county-level campaign finance record may be held locally by Ellis County or may not be indexed online in an easy public-search form.

## Action Evidence

### 2024 re-election

Official Ellis County result: Scott J. Braun, Republican, 11,797 votes for sheriff; write-ins, 117. Hays Post preliminary election-night result: 11,524. Use the official number.

### Jan. 20, 2026 Tyler public-safety cloud / jail-management system

Official minutes say Communications Director Jeff Ridgway and Sheriff Scott Braun asked commissioners to authorize a Tyler Technologies cloud migration and SaaS agreement for public-safety software. The minutes say the agreement includes an upgrade for sheriff's office jail-management software, identifies the existing JMS as nearing end-of-life, and describes expected data-flow/compatibility benefits. Braun is recorded as saying he understood it was a "big ask" but the system replacement was inevitable. Commissioners approved PO 7984 for $348,076 by a 2-0 voice vote.

### May 8, 2025 LEC remodel / jail planning

Official retreat minutes say Braun discussed when the Hays Police Department might move out of the Law Enforcement Center, estimated in 2026, and brought floor plans for remodel discussion. The minutes record his view that the LEC remodel would not address possible future jail needs. No action was taken.

### HB 2771 ICE-detainer testimony

The official Kansas Legislature bill page lists Braun as a Feb. 25, 2026 oral proponent witness on HB 2771, a bill addressing county sheriffs' enforcement of ICE detainers, insurance coverage, state payment of certain judgments, attorney-general representation, and sheriff authority to enter ICE-related interlocal agreements. The bill died. Hays Post reports Braun told the committee Ellis County signed an ICE memorandum partly because of federal-funding risk and supported HB 2771 for liability and taxpayer-protection reasons.

### Opioid / fentanyl training and AEDs

Hays Post reported on Apr. 14, 2025 that Braun would ask commissioners to approve about $800 from the opioid settlement fund for travel costs for a retired narcotics detective to teach local law enforcement about fentanyl and opioid-overdose death investigations. Official Nov. 5, 2024 minutes also record Braun asking approval to use opioid funds to purchase AEDs.

## Candidate-Attributed Issue Statements

### Jail and public safety

Braun repeatedly frames jail capacity as a practical public-safety issue. In Dec. 2023 Hays Post coverage, he explained that the jail's nominal 72-bed capacity cannot always be fully used because of isolation, witness separation, inmate behavior, gender, offense levels, and COVID-era effects. In July 2024 coverage, he said the county needed a jail even though he did not personally want one, and tied the issue to public safety, mental health, and long-range planning.

### ICE detainers and limits

In Nov. 2025 Hays Post coverage, Braun said Ellis County's ICE agreement applies to people already arrested and booked on local charges, that ICE has 48 hours after local custody ends, and that the sheriff's office would not hold people beyond that window. He also said the office does not have the same 287(g) task-force authority as KBI and would not stop people in the street for immigration investigations.

### Drug forfeiture

In Jan. 2024 Hays Post coverage, Braun argued as KSA president that forfeiture funds support equipment and drug-enforcement operations and that losing them would shift costs to taxpayers. He opposed a fixed cash threshold for seizures, saying a smaller amount might matter differently in a small community than a metro area.

### Mental health

Braun told Hays Post in Jan. 2024 that every Kansas sheriff was struggling with mental-health issues and questioned whether people with serious mental illness belonged in county jail facilities. In July 2025 budget coverage, he supported High Plains Mental Health crisis-intervention funding as helping the sheriff's office and other local agencies, with the jail not being the best place for people needing mental-health care.

### Public communication

In Jan. 2025 app coverage, Braun said the sheriff's office needed a direct communication channel for road/weather/public-safety information instead of relying only on third-party platforms. In March 2025 scam-warning coverage, he said official sheriff communications about warrants or court appearances do not come as unsolicited payment demands.
