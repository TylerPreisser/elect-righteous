## Issue-Mapped Findings

### Official role / election status

- City of Hays official pages identify Shaun Musil as a member of the current City Commission.
- Hays Post reported Musil, Sandy Jacobs, and David Vilaysing were sworn in on January 8, 2026 after the November 2025 election.
- Hays Post reported Musil had served on the commission for 12 years, had lived in Hays for 30 years, and worked as an account representative for Nex-Tech.
- Nex-Tech's public profile says Musil has more than 30 years of business/sales experience, works with managed IT/cybersecurity/physical security/communications solutions, has small-business-owner background, and serves as a Hays city commissioner.

**Mapping note:** Use as metadata and office-status issue card. Avoid placing him in a 2026 election guide as if voters will see his name on the 2026 ballot.

### Water security / R9 Ranch

- Musil's strongest recurring issue is the R9 Ranch long-term water project.
- In the 2025 Hays Post Q&A, he said he was running because of "unfinished business," mainly the water project, and expected at least the approval process to be over in the next three or four years.
- In the 2025 candidate forum, he said "I believe it's going to happen," said Hays was not taking from Kinsley, and said Hays had the state's lowest per-person water usage.
- In 2024, after an administrative law judge ruled favorably on the water transfer application, Musil called it a huge win for Hays, Russell, and the region, said conservation would continue, and emphasized being good neighbors to Kinsley.
- The same 2024 coverage reported the project cost estimate at $140 million and described the transfer-panel review process.

**Mapping note:** Strong stated-position evidence, with official city project/action context through R9 agenda packets. No clean individual roll-call dissent surfaced; Musil appears aligned with the commission's long-term R9 push.

### Housing

- In the 2025 Q&A, Musil called relaxed RHID policy one of the most impactful commission actions in recent years because it helped get more houses built, while acknowledging the mix of housing still did not fully match the city's need.
- At the 2025 forum, he said developers had sought city letters of support for affordable-housing applications but had been turned down by state/federal agencies, and said the goal was to build more houses so some existing housing becomes more affordable.
- City agenda materials through April 2026 show continued commission work around development incentives and housing-related fee waivers, but the most quote-rich Musil-specific evidence is Hays Post.

**Mapping note:** Stated position is pro-supply and practical; he does not present a simple low-income-housing fix in the accessible record.

### Economic development / retail / sales-tax base

- Musil repeatedly links retail development to city finances and growth.
- In 2021, he called Grow Hays the best economic-development program since he had been on the commission and said Hays could not go backwards.
- In 2024, he said city-owned land north of I-70 showed Hays was serious about development and that retail was a priority; he also floated a convention center at that location.
- In 2026 Chick-fil-A incentive coverage, he credited Grow Hays and city staff and asked, "Chick-fil-A now; what's next?"
- 2026 Grow Hays annual-review coverage described Musil as pleased with 2025 economic-development momentum.

**Mapping note:** Strong candidate-attributed pattern. Use incentive votes only where minutes are primary-anchored; Hays Post supports the narrative.

### Budget / taxes / nonprofits

- In 2023 budget coverage, Musil said lowering the mill levy was easier said than done, recognized cost pressure on residents and business owners, and wanted efficiencies without putting a future commission in trouble.
- In 2025 Q&A, he said he had no thought of raising the mill levy in the next few years but "you never want to say never."
- At the 2025 forum, he said funding local nonprofits at a higher level would probably require a mill levy increase.
- In April 2026 budget-prep coverage, he worried that outside nonprofit agencies would ask for more funding and asked what would happen if city support fell and services disappeared.
- KPDC says its Campaign Finance Act coverage applies to county, first-class city, Wichita school board, and Kansas City BPU local offices. Hays is not a first-class city, so local city-candidate finance records may not appear in KPDC's scanned state repository.

**Mapping note:** Position is cautious, service-continuity focused, and not anti-tax absolutist. Donor/funding absence is partly structural, not necessarily noncompliance.

### Public health / COVID-era governance

- During the August 27, 2020 mask-ordinance extension, Hays Post reported the commission voted 3-2 to extend the ordinance; Musil, then mayor, supported enforcement and said his goal was to keep schools and businesses open.
- On October 2, 2020, Hays Post reported a 3-1 extension vote: Berges, Musil, and Jacobs yes; Mason Ruder no; Ron Mellick absent.
- In January 2021 reorganization coverage, Musil thanked city staff and his wife, said his family had faced difficult things during the pandemic year, and the article reported all five Musil family members had experienced coronavirus and quarantines.
- Existing local memory records vandalism/threats directed at his family vehicles after mask conflict, but the strongest directly reviewed public pages in this pass anchor the conflict, family strain, and business impacts rather than a full primary police-record trail.

**Mapping note:** Treat as a documented public-health governance action and pressure test. Do not overstate the threat/vandalism details without the exact source ID in downstream prose.

### Infrastructure / airport / public facilities

- Musil defended Essential Air Service funding in May 2025, saying federal air support was not wasteful spending and helped keep Hays relevant for doctors, Fort Hays State University international students, and regional access.
- At the 2025 forum he said parks should be reprioritized and the city should examine what it maintains and puts into parks.
- The 2026 Chick-fil-A article also records preventative sewer-maintenance discussion at the same work session, but Musil's named quote in that story is on business recruitment rather than sewer policy.

**Mapping note:** Strongest infrastructure quote is airport/EAS. Parks and public facilities are secondary stated priorities.

### Business / community identity

- FHSU's 2018 coverage identifies Musil as a Paisley Pear co-owner with his wife and quotes him welcoming students downtown.
- Hays Post's January 2021 coverage says Shaun and Heather Musil co-owned the Paisley Pear, which was affected by Kansas pandemic shutdowns.
- Existing local memory says the Paisley Pear was later sold in 2024; do not present that sale as newly re-confirmed in this pass unless separately sourced.
- Nex-Tech's profile presents his small-business background as part of his professional pitch to educational and governmental customers.

**Mapping note:** Useful for "who they are" and conflict-awareness context, but no formal ethics complaint or improper vote surfaced in reviewed sources.
