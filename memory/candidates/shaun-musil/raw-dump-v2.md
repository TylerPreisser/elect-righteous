## Executive Summary

Shaun Musil's public record is local-government heavy and issue-specific. He has served on the Hays City Commission for roughly 12 years, works as an account representative for Nex-Tech, previously co-owned the Paisley Pear with his wife, Heather, and was re-elected in 2025 as one of the two four-year Hays City Commission winners.

The record is dominated by water security, especially the R9 Ranch project; pro-growth economic development and retail recruitment; housing supply; careful budget language; pandemic-era public-health conflict; and airport/infrastructure concerns. No current church affiliation, campaign website, detailed donor record, or inspectable public social feed was found.

There are two important corrections for downstream use. First, Hays City Commission is an odd-year local cycle, and Musil should not be shown as if he is running in 2026. Second, the official Ellis County results PDF appears to list Musil at 1,788 votes, while Hays Post's election-night/unofficial reporting used 1,772. Treat 1,788 as the official number after PDF/OCR confirmation.

## Issue Research

### Water security / R9 Ranch

**Findings**

- 2025 Q&A: Musil said the main reason he was running was unfinished business, mainly the water project.
- 2025 Q&A: He expected that in the next three or four years, the approval process would at least be over.
- 2025 forum: Incumbents Barrick, Jacobs, and Musil all said they were running in part to see the R9 line go online.
- 2025 forum: Musil said he believed R9 would happen, that Hays was not taking away from Kinsley, and that Hays had the lowest per-person water usage rate in Kansas.
- 2024 R9 ruling: Hays Post reported an administrative law judge issued a favorable initial order on the transfer application.
- 2024 R9 ruling: Musil, then mayor, called the ruling a huge win for Hays, Russell, and the region; said conservation would continue; and emphasized being good neighbors to Kinsley.
- 2024 R9 ruling: The article described an estimated $140 million project and transfer-panel review.

### Housing

**Findings**

- 2025 Q&A: Musil said relaxing the RHID policy had allowed more people to use it, resulting in more houses being built.
- 2025 Q&A: He said the frustrating part was that the city still was not getting the exact kind of houses it needed.
- 2025 forum: Musil said several development groups had asked the city for letters of support for federal or state affordable-housing funds, but those developers had been turned down by the agencies.
- 2025 forum: Musil said the city goal was to build more houses, hopefully opening up more affordable housing.
- 2025 forum: He said staff was working on affordable-housing planning, but it was harder than it seemed.

### Budget / taxes / city services

**Findings**

- 2023 budget coverage in existing local harvest: Musil said lowering the mill levy was easier said than done and that he wanted efficiencies without putting future commissioners in a dire position.
- 2025 Q&A: Musil said he had no thought of raising the mill levy in the next few years, but "you never want to say never."
- 2025 forum: Musil said funding local nonprofits at a higher amount would probably require a mill levy increase.
- 2026 budget prep: Musil worried outside nonprofit agencies would ask for more money and asked what happens if the city does not give it to them and they stop providing current services.
- Hays Post 2026 budget prep reported the general fund is primarily funded by the city local sales tax and noted cost pressures including chemicals, insurance, software, vehicles, tariffs, and property-tax policy uncertainty.
