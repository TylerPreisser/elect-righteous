## Candidate Statements And In-Their-Own-Words Evidence

2021 vacancy application:

- Hays Post published applicant letters as submitted.
- Yarmer said educating children is one of parents' most important responsibilities and described wanting to be active in his daughters' education and other district students' education.
- He tied his emergency-medicine work to pandemic-era school decisions, saying he had frontline healthcare experience and access to medical literature.
- He also cited nearly 10 years of construction-industry experience as relevant to district infrastructure decisions.

2023 candidate Q&A:

- Bio: age 39, ER doctor, children in USD 489 schools, not an incumbent at the time.
- Funding statement: "100 percent personally financed."
- Bond theme: wanted residents to receive the promised schools at the promised price.
- Transparency/process: asked for all meetings to be broadcast, agendas at least a week ahead, and easier board-member agenda additions.
- Board role: oversight of broad goals, spending, and what students are taught; not day-to-day management.
- Bond responsibility: said the board ultimately had responsibility to keep the $143.5 million project on budget.

2025 published written statement:

- Hays Post says Yarmer responded by email and the statement was included in full.
- Core claim: district was using capital outlay for items he believed should be under the bond.
- Specific criticized priorities: concession stand, scoreboards, tennis courts, marquee, while other building needs remained deferred.
- He wrote that board oversight had failed and used the phrase "reckless spending."

2025 special education / SEL discussion:

- Hays Post attributed comments that the district could "work a little leaner."
- He questioned whether requested items were needs.
- He said he did not think money was the education system's core problem and criticized "social-emotional" school programming.
- He emphasized core subjects including "math, science and history."

Parent rights / emergency policy:

- Hays Post attributed to Yarmer the statement: "I don't think you give up your parental rights between 8 and 3."
- Treat as a direct public parental-rights signal, but do not overstate beyond the emergency-policy context.
