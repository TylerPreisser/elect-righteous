## Bottom Line

Derek Yarmer's public profile is local and operational rather than campaign-media driven. The strongest evidence is not a candidate website or social feed; it is a combination of official USD 489 HighBond/Diligent minutes, Hays Post candidate questionnaires and a published written statement, plus contemporaneous Hays Post and Tiger Media meeting coverage.

## Issue Map

### Bond And Capital Spending Oversight

The clearest issue lane. Yarmer stated in 2023 that the board should make sure Hays residents received the schools promised at the price promised. In 2025, he criticized capital-outlay money being used for items he said belonged under the bond and wrote that board oversight had failed.

Official votes line up with that posture: he moved the March 2025 motion to look into an outside bond project manager; voted no on several athletic/furniture/playground approvals; voted no on the Sep. 22, 2025 Hays High change-order motion; and voted no on the Feb. 23, 2026 Felten GMP.

### Board Process, Agendas, Procurement, And Contracts

Yarmer's process critique is consistent from campaign to board record. He asked for meeting broadcasts, earlier agendas, easier board agenda additions, and clearer oversight boundaries. Official minutes show abstention on Jan. 22, 2024 administrator extensions, no/table posture on Feb. 2026 HVAC and contract matters, and support for tabling contract language before later votes in March 2026.

Tiger Media and Hays Post add the attributed quotes behind the votes: same-night numbers were a recurring objection.

### Core Academics And School Scope

The record here is more statement-heavy than vote-heavy. His 2021 application framed education first as a parental responsibility. Hays Post's Feb. 20, 2025 coverage records him questioning whether district requests were needs, saying the district could work leaner, and criticizing expansive social-emotional/mental-health school roles while emphasizing core subjects.

This is a real signal but not a full curriculum platform. No detailed written position was found on standards, testing, library policy, vouchers, teacher unions, or state education finance formulas.

### Parent Rights, Safety, And Facilities

Yarmer's public statements include a direct parental-rights line during emergency-policy coverage and practical safety concerns around bathroom/vandalism monitoring. Official Apr. 20, 2026 minutes show he seconded the motion to add HHS bathroom discussion to the agenda. The same pattern also appears in facilities safety questions on asbestos, HVAC, and Felten project scoping.

Do not overstate this into a nationalized culture-war profile. The public record is mostly local facilities, safety, and process.
