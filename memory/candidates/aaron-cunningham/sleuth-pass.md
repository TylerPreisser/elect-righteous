## Issue-Mapped Observations

### Observation 1 - Off-cycle incumbent status is verified

**What was observed:** Ellis County lists Cunningham as County Attorney from 2025 to present. Kansas statute gives county attorneys four-year terms. Ellis County's 2026 filing PDF does not include County Attorney as a current race. The 2024 official-results source path plus readable result copies show Cunningham won the 2024 county attorney election.

**Issue mapping:** Official status / election-cycle accuracy. This is the most important correction for the v2 profile: Cunningham should remain in the off-cycle incumbents grouping, not the 2026 Ellis County ballot.

**Source tier:** Primary for county office page, statute, election-results page/PDF, and 2026 filing PDF; readable vote totals are supported by secondary/search-OCR and IKE Lab.

### Observation 2 - Office priorities emphasize serious crimes, child victims, violent/property crime, drug distribution, and efficiency

**What was observed:** The official county attorney page and 2025 county budget book state the office's goals as prioritizing cases involving death, child victims including CINC cases, sex crimes, violent person crimes, property crimes, and drug distribution crimes; effectively prosecuting as many cases as possible; and maximizing office efficiency for taxpayers.

**Issue mapping:** Prosecution priorities / office capacity / taxpayer stewardship.

**Source tier:** Primary.

### Observation 3 - Cunningham changed the office's marijuana enforcement posture after taking office

**What was observed:** Hays Post reported on February 17, 2025 that Cunningham would resume charging some marijuana cases after predecessor Robert Anderson had not filed certain small-possession cases. Cunningham framed this as enforcing Kansas law as written and leaving legalization/decriminalization choices to the Legislature. He also said limited resources require prioritization and that a nonlocal traveler with only a marijuana pipe may not be prosecuted if not an imminent local threat.

**Issue mapping:** Marijuana/drug enforcement; role of county attorney; prosecutorial discretion and resource limits.

**Source tier:** Secondary, with direct candidate quotes.

### Observation 4 - Delta-8 / Delta-10 should be attributed carefully

**What was observed:** A January 2022 Hays Post Delta-8 article was about then-County Attorney Robert Anderson, not Cunningham. Cunningham's own February 2025 Hays Post interview says he would consider filing charges on hemp-derived products such as Delta-8 and Delta-10. Do not describe the 2022 business-warning action as Cunningham's action.

**Issue mapping:** Drug enforcement; source hygiene.

**Source tier:** Secondary.

### Observation 5 - Victim/witness services are both an office function and an early staffing action

**What was observed:** Ellis County's January 2025 commission packet records Cunningham, as newly elected county attorney, asking commissioners to approve a Victim Witness Coordinator job description. The motion passed 3-0. The official Victim Information page says the office notifies victims of hearings, handles restitution requests, and receives reports from law enforcement for charging decisions.

**Issue mapping:** Victim services; office operations; staffing.

**Source tier:** Primary.

### Observation 6 - Rural legal capacity and defense-counsel shortages are a recurring public theme

**What was observed:** Hays Post reported Cunningham's comments that the office usually had four attorneys handling about 1,500 law-enforcement reports per year, that Kansas is not producing enough attorneys or incentivizing them to stay rural, and that a shortage of qualified defense counsel slows cases and leaves defendants waiting in jail. Kansas Courts' Rural Justice Initiative materials also list Cunningham in rural-justice committee work in existing local harvest.

**Issue mapping:** Rural legal workforce; court delay; jail pressure; public defense capacity.

**Source tier:** Secondary for interview; primary/official for Kansas Courts committee roster where available.

### Observation 7 - Public case evidence shows a formal-systems argument against retaliation

**What was observed:** In a March 26, 2026 Hays Post sentencing story, Cunningham opposed probation for a defendant convicted of aggravated assault on a law enforcement officer and criminal threat. The article reports he argued the court should not allow justification based on allegations against another person and said probation would signal that people could bypass investigative and court systems.

**Issue mapping:** Public safety; formal legal process; violent-threat sentencing.

**Source tier:** Secondary.

### Observation 8 - Community/public safety appearances exist, but not a broad campaign platform

**What was observed:** HaysMed listed Cunningham as part of a June 27, 2025 human-trafficking awareness panel with medical, law enforcement, and retired KBI participants. KWCH reported in February 2026 that Cunningham confirmed a second-degree murder conviction in a Hays infant methamphetamine-overdose case. These are professional/public safety appearances, not campaign issue pages.

**Issue mapping:** Public safety; human trafficking awareness; drug-related fatality prosecution.

**Source tier:** Secondary.
