## Primary-Source Leads By Issue

### Appointed Administration / Role Scope

- City staff profile: appointed city manager since Aug. 1, 2007; assistant city manager since March 2005; prior city administrator in Gallatin, Missouri; transportation-industry work before city management; KU political science/public service/civic leadership and Certified Public Manager background.
- City Commission page: Hays uses commission-manager government; commission hires the city manager; city manager advises commissioners, enforces policy, prepares proposed budget, and appoints department heads.
- City Manager's Office page: city management staff administer policies established by the City Commission across municipal operations.

### R9 Ranch / Water Supply / Litigation

- KDA Hays/Russell change-application page says Hays bought the approximately 7,000-acre R9 Ranch and 30 water rights in 1995 for future public water supply; chief engineer contingently approved change applications on March 27, 2019; litigation and appeal history followed.
- Hays Post January 2026 coverage says pipeline and wellfield designs were complete, 115 of 130 easements had been acquired, and the city awaited a Kansas Supreme Court ruling after December 2025 arguments.
- March 2026 Hays Post coverage of HB 2433 quoted Dougherty arguing state law controls water appropriations and transfers and that county zoning should not duplicate or usurp state water law.
- HPPR/KLC Journal May 2026 coverage framed the R9 fight as a survival issue for Hays and Russell while including Edwards County/WaterPACK concerns. Dougherty described the project as existential and said Hays and Russell had complied and more than complied.

### R9 Ranch Wildfire / Land Stewardship

- Hays Post/Kansas Reflector May 7, 2026 coverage reported a 1,500-acre Edwards County wildfire after an April 15 controlled burn on city-owned R9 land; the Kansas State Fire Marshal listed the cause as undetermined and did not draw a connection between the two fires.
- The same story quoted Dougherty saying the city had not yet seen the state fire marshal final report and the city-retained investigator had not completed a report, so no further comment would be made until both were received and reviewed.
- Treat this as an unresolved operations/stewardship issue, not a finding of causation.

### Budget / Service Levels / Fiscal Management

- Hays Post March 2025 retreat coverage quoted Dougherty saying the city did not have much "fluff" and takes care of what it has.
- Hays Post June 2025 coverage quoted Dougherty describing the 2026 preliminary budget as maintenance-focused and non-expansionary, citing inflation, federal grant uncertainty, tariff/recession concerns, and vehicle costs.
- Hays Post April 2026 coverage of 2027 budget planning quoted Dougherty saying Hays has maintained high service standards but must reduce some service level if the current standard is not sustainable.
- Hays Post September 2024 revenue-neutral coverage captured Dougherty's argument that static property-tax collections are impractical when city costs increase.

### Infrastructure / Economic Development / Staff Administration

- Official commission packets show regular staff requests, budget materials, board appointments, industrial revenue bond items, audits, and financial statements routed through the city process.
- Hays Post April 2026 coverage described travel-plaza incentives and budget preparation; Dougherty called for an executive session for attorney-client consultation with city officials.
- Hays Post May 15, 2026 City of Hays release announced Michelle "Nikki" Brown as CVB director and quoted Dougherty on tourism, local business activity, and the city's broader profile.
- Strong Towns materials from 2016 identify Dougherty as a city-manager voice around Hays' infrastructure, finance, public conversation, and community-planning posture.

### Water Conservation / Reuse

- KCUR/Kansas News Service 2022 coverage quoted Dougherty saying Hays had looked to Las Vegas, Tucson, Phoenix, and Utah for water-conservation ideas. The article described cash-for-grass, demonstration gardens, landscaping rules, wastewater reuse study, and customer conservation tools.
- Existing local harvest also captured city-submitted Strong Towns material describing conservation, effluent reuse, inclining water rates, boards/commissions, downtown reinvestment, and sewer/street data.
